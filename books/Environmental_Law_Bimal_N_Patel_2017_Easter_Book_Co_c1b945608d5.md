Preface | 
Eazy Cases on Environmental Law attempts to provide in a simple 
and succinct legal language brief analysis of 87 important environmental 
cases that have been examined by the Indian judiciary. How the Indian 
Judiciary has effectively and decisively read into emerging principles 
of international environmental law post 1972 Human Environment 
Conference and 1992 Rio Conference, has been the main focus of this 
short editorial work researched by the GNLU students. Although the 
judiciary has been subject to healthy and unfounded criticisms, the 
fact remains that it has successfully used international environmental 
principles in accordance with socio-economic growing condition of the 
nation. It requires no gainsay that it is because of the active role taken 
by the judiciary that the common man and woman of India is able to 
seek several redresses through the implementation of these principles.’ 
Its role in appraising the environmental consciousness of the people of 
India is immeasurable. It may not be out of place to even argue that the 
environmental principles which evolved in the Stockholm and Rio dec- 
laration have been implemented in the domestic laws due to the vibrant 
role played by the judiciary. Not only the judiciary has clarified various 
emerging and existing principles of environmental law but the flipside of 
the coin is that it has treaded into the territory of legislative through its 
judicial activism in the area of environmental law. In fact, the judiciary 
have been using environmental and human rights cases, since 1970s, ina 
most tactful manner to expand its judicial outreach, encroachment into 
legislative and executive domains, to the much dislike of the legislature 
and executive. It is seen as a strategy to exaggerate its jurisdiction. This 
work shows: how some environmental cases have been interpreted by 
the judiciary without taking into consideration the consequences of its 
interpretation, making the Supreme Court an activist court instead of

Analysis of Article 21 related cases proves how the judiciary has taken 
recourse to enlarge powers of legislative interference by way of article 
21. While this is a matter of appreciation, from a strict constitutional 
point, the judiciary in the process has obliterated all lines of jurisdiction. 
Environmental cases have led to phenomena whereby even letters by 
strangers and newspapers reports are used to start environmental litiga- 
tion. Bureaucracy has found some respite in the environmentally activist 
judiciary. Judicial orders have helped the bureaucracy to an extent in 
holding them not accountable for their decisions as they have been wait- 
ing for court’s order for all environmental problems. It is apt to recollect 
what Professor Upendra Baxi once said, activism has brought extraor- 
dinary power to the Indian appellate judiciary as well an agendum of 
acute anxieties. These arise because justices are loath to lose their new 
found power.’ This is certainly true for environmental law and practices 
in India.
